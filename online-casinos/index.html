The four gates a casino passes before we score it

Not criteria. Gates. Fail one and there is no review.

  1. It must pay us, from our own funded account

    We deposit our own money, play, and request a withdrawal. A brand that does not pay is not reviewed, not listed and not linked. This is a gate rather than a scoring criterion, because there is no score that compensates for not being paid.

  2. It must accept a working New Zealand payment route

    A casino that technically accepts New Zealand registrations but declines every card a New Zealand bank issues is not usable, however good the lobby looks.

  3. It must state its wagering terms in full before you deposit

    Multiplier, basis, game weighting, maximum bet and maximum cashout. We cannot price an offer we cannot read, and an operator unwilling to publish the basis is telling you something.

  4. It must carry an age check and a working self-exclusion tool

    R18 enforcement and a deposit limit that can be set in the account rather than by emailing support. A site that makes limits hard to set has made a choice about who it is for.

Banking from a New Zealand account

Three things cost New Zealanders money here, and only one of them is ever discussed.

1. The currency you are holding

Ten of the fifteen casinos here hold New Zealand dollars end to end. The rest hold euros, and a euro balance costs you a conversion spread twice — going in and coming out — totalling around 4.8% of the round trip. On NZ$1,000 that is about NZ$48. It never appears as a fee; it appears as a smaller number arriving than the one you expected. Over a year it comfortably exceeds the difference between any two welcome offers on this page.

2. Whether your bank will allow the transaction

Several major New Zealand banks now decline gambling merchant category codes on credit cards as policy, and some extend it to debit. A declined deposit is usually your bank rather than the casino. POLi remains the most reliable New Zealand-specific route, Neosurf covers players who would rather not connect a bank account at all, and crypto bypasses the question entirely.

3. The weekly cap, which is not a banking detail but behaves like one

Every site here except Spino caps weekly withdrawals, between €4,000 and NZ$10,000. That figure decides how long a large win takes to reach you, no matter how fast the payout rail is. What the 2027 licensing transition means for your account

The market these casinos operate in is about to change shape, and it is worth understanding before you open an account you intend to keep.

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 commenced on 1 May 2026. It creates a licensed domestic market administered by the Department of Internal Affairs, with up to 15 licences. Applications close on 1 December 2026 — 71 days from today — and the first licensed sites are expected during 2027.

What improves

A licensed operator will be bound by New Zealand harm-minimisation rules, a domestic complaints route and DIA oversight. For a player that is a materially stronger position than any offshore licence offers, and it is the first time New Zealanders will have somewhere local to escalate.

What to expect from the offshore sites

Some will apply for a licence and some will not. Those that do not will continue operating as they do now — the Act binds operators rather than players, so your account does not become unlawful. But the advertising and affiliate restrictions arriving with the licensed market mean the information environment around them will thin out considerably.
